About RainVision Platform

Comprehensive documentation of architecture, dataset engineering, and academic methodology.

🎯 Problem Statement

Rainfall prediction is a critical challenge in meteorological science, agriculture, and municipal flood disaster management. Traditional numerical weather prediction (NWP) models require extensive supercomputing infrastructure. RainVision demonstrates that machine learning pipelines trained on localized atmospheric parameters can yield high-precision continuous rainfall forecasts in milliseconds.

📊 Dataset & Meteorological Features

Trained on 15,279 daily meteorological records (1985 to 2026). Features include:

  • Ambient Temperature (°C): Thermodynamic indicator of atmospheric energy.
  • Relative Humidity (%): Moisture saturation level of the air column.
  • Barometric Pressure (hPa): Barometric pressure drops signal storm systems.
  • Wind Speed (km/h): Advection speed of moisture-laden air masses.
  • Previous Day Rainfall (mm): Temporal autocorrelation of rainy fronts.

🛡 Preprocessing & Feature Engineering

To prevent data leakage, temporal lag features are constructed strictly from antecedent observations:

rain_lag_1, 2, 3 rain_roll_mean_7 rain_roll_max_7 pressure_diff_1 sin_day_of_year cos_day_of_year is_monsoon

🤖 Machine Learning Models

Two complementary models are evaluated side-by-side:

Default: Blended Ensemble: Combines a Random Forest and Gradient Boosting model.
Baselines: Linear Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, and two-stage Hurdle models are also available.

📐 Evaluation Metrics Explained

$R^2$ Score: Ensemble: 0.5228; RF: -0.4405; LR: 0.1499 on the 546-record holdout.
MAE: Ensemble: 5.932 mm; RF: 7.845 mm; LR: 7.715 mm.
RMSE: Ensemble: 11.260 mm; RF: 19.563 mm; LR: 15.029 mm.
